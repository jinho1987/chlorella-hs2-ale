Sanger validation of the ALE candidates: first wet-lab results

The 15 Sanger/PCR assays from §11 have now been run once. One missense candidate (g8767) is confirmed and two repeat-bound intronic SNPs are partly confirmed. At three loci with clean traces, the ALE DNA used reads like HS2 rather than like the sequenced ALE colony, so the origin of that template is being checked before any of the negative calls are interpreted. Everything below is stated against the 2026 PacBio HiFi data only.

Bottom line
  • Confirmed: g8767 (L82P), the strongest candidate. SCP46 and RIMO are confirmed only partly, because both sit at repeat boundaries. g5108/D344 is a confirmatory identity match, not a SNP.
  • Not confirmed, with a specific reason: the ALE traces for g1283, g5108 and g4421 look like HS2 (peak-height ratios unchanged), although the long reads show those alleles at 94–100% in the sequenced ALE colony. The g4421 locus is clean and repeat-free, so this is a template question, not a reading problem.
  • The validation rate is therefore thin: one clean confirmation (g8767) out of 13. We do not treat the other results as evidence against the HiFi calls until the ALE DNA source is settled.
  • One design error of ours was found (the EPHA reverse primer is a CA repeat), and replacement primers are listed below. None of them has been tested at the bench.

  • The HS2 traces behave as the long reads predict: g1283 and g5108 are heterozygous in HS2.
  • The ALE traces are indistinguishable from HS2 at all three loci. If the template carried the sequenced colony’s alleles, the g1283 and g5108 ratios should shift sharply toward alt; they do not move (40% vs 40%, 72% vs 73%).
  • g4421 is the cleanest case. It has no repeat nearby and the best-supported HiFi difference in the set (every HS2 read is C, every ALE read is T, and every amplifiable ALE assembly copy carries T). The ALE trace shows no T at all, so a sequencing artifact or a mixed sample cannot explain it.
  • Interpretation: three independent loci agree that the ALE template in these reactions carries the HS2 alleles. That is a statement about the template, not about the HiFi calls or the primers. The same DNA did show the expected ALE allele at g8767. The origin of the ALE DNA (colony, culture, passage) is being established, and DNA from the PacBio colony would be the decisive control.
03

Repeat-bound loci and what a negative can mean

Several SNPs sit at the end of homopolymer or dinucleotide repeats, where Sanger reads degrade and a one-base change can be indistinguishable from polymerase slippage.

  • g4523: the ±300 bp around the SNP is 70% GC and 38% low-complexity or GC-rich; the SNP sits inside a 62 bp block of (GT)n, an 18-G run and GC-rich sequence. There is no clean site for a PCR or sequencing primer within ±220 bp, so redesign is not possible and the locus is dropped from the Sanger list. The HiFi evidence stands.
  • SCP46, RIMO, CLPB3, TLP13: all repeat-adjacent. SCP46 and RIMO were seen but only partly informative; RIMO is only 37% alt in the sequenced ALE colony, and CLPB3 and TLP13 reads are 15–19% ambiguous in HiFi too. For these, a failure to confirm is not a refutation.
  • g1283 and g5108 are frequency shifts of an allele HS2 already carries (49% and 48% in HS2), so “same base called” is the wrong test; the peak-height ratio is the right one.

HiFi triage of all 24 candidates found only three that are clean, single-copy, fixed novel SNPs and not yet tried: ptg000005l:14125, g8143 and g6058. They were set aside as optional control loci, not mechanistic targets (both genes are intronic with no Swiss-Prot hit).

04

The two structural-variant assays

  • 16 kb duplication junction (#14). No band appeared in ALE, and none is expected in HS2. The original primer pair matches the ALE assembly exactly (397 bp) and 127 uniquely mapped HiFi reads span the whole junction at 135× depth, so the duplication is real in the sequenced colony. The junction sits at ALE ptg000030l:209,756. The old reverse primer also carried a GTGTGT repeat. Two repeat-free replacement pairs are below. They should be run in the same tube as the g8767 pair, so that HS2 always shows 479 bp and ALE shows 479 bp plus the junction band: a control-only ALE lane means the duplication is absent from that DNA, and no control band means a PCR or template failure. Given the g1283 result above, an ALE lane without the junction band would fit the template question rather than a missing duplication.
  • Insertion junction (#15). Bands appear, but the 17.5 kb (HS2) and 19.0 kb (ALE) products exceed the 10 kb ladder. A ladder reaching beyond 20 kb and a low-percentage, low-voltage gel are needed, and the 8.5% size difference will be subtle. The first and last 900 bp readable from each primer are on file for sequencing.
05

Primer problems found and replacement designs

One genuine design error surfaced: the EPHA reverse primer (CACACACACCACACACACAC) is a CA repeat with about 1,000 near-matches genome-wide, which explains the multiband result. Our earlier uniqueness audit tested exact matches only and missed it. The replacement designs were screened for off-target products at up to 3 mismatches per primer in the HS2 and ALE purged and unpurged assemblies, and for variants at the primer sites in the HiFi reads (at most 2%). None has been tested at the bench.

Because the old UBP12 and ACOX4 primers had no predicted off-target product, their multibands may come from repeats inside the amplicons, and replacement helps only if the primers were the cause. New primers cannot change a genotype call, so they will not resolve the template question.

06

Open questions and next steps

  1. Settle the ALE DNA source: which colony or culture it came from and whether it matches the PacBio colony. The most informative test uses primers already in hand: re-extract DNA from the PacBio colony stock (or several single colonies of the current ALE culture) and run g4421 and g8767. If g4421 then reads T, the DNA source was the issue.
  2. Collect the remaining traces: g8767, SCP46 and RIMO, to confirm those three results are clean (the raw trace for g1283 reverse, g5108 and g4421 reverse reads would also help).
  3. Rerun #14 with pair A or B plus the g8767 control; get the larger ladder for #15.
  4. For the manuscript: report g8767 as the one clean Sanger confirmation, describe the repeat-bound loci as repeat-limited rather than refuted, and hold any claim about the other candidates until the template question is closed.
