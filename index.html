01 — The experiment

One strain, one stressor, two genomes to compare

Chlorella sp. HS2 is a green, freshwater-tolerant microalga. ALE is its own descendant, produced by prolonged culturing under hydrolysate — the dark, phenolic-and-furan-rich liquid left over after breaking down lignocellulosic biomass, and normally hostile to microbial growth. We assembled both genomes independently from PacBio HiFi long reads and compared them directly, rather than mapping short reads onto a single reference and inferring the rest.

02 — Phenotype

Eighteen months of selection produced a measurably different alga

Before asking what changed in the genome, here is what changed in the flask: a clean, dose-dependent growth advantage under hydrolysate stress, a plausible light-linked mechanism behind it, and a consistent biochemical and single-cell signature across seven independent measurement types. 05 — Convergent evolution

Two other lineages, evolved under different stress

HS2 is the shared ancestor of three independently-selected lines, not just ALE. HS19 was selected under sodium hypochlorite (oxidative-chlorine) stress; HMF was selected against 5-hydroxymethylfurfural alone — the specific furanic sub-component of ALE's full hydrolysate cocktail. Comparing all three asks whether unrelated selective pressures converge on the same biology. An initial single-caller comparison found three exact-position matches; none survived re-calling with the same rigor applied everywhere else in this project, and were retracted as convergence evidence rather than kept for a good headline.

07 — Temporal trajectory (preliminary)

Everything above is a direct, single-timepoint comparison between the HS2 and ALE genomes as they exist today, from this year's long-read sequencing. It's included here for context, not as part of the primary comparison; extending it properly is planned as its own future study.

09 — A decade of background

Is the ALE signal bigger than normal drift?

A 2015 PacBio assembly of HS2 itself — the same lineage, eleven years earlier, before the ALE experiment existed — gives a rare chance to ask what "normal" looks like: how much does this genome drift under nothing but routine lab culture, and does any of that background noise touch the ALE findings above?

11 — Genome engineering targets

Where to build next: candidate landing-pad loci

ALE's advantage — and its lipid-storage bias in particular — is a natural starting point for further metabolic engineering (e.g. pathway genes to push lipid accumulation further). That means identifying landing pads: genomic sites where a construct can be inserted without disrupting anything that matters, and without landing in an unstable or ambiguous region of the assembly.

We screened the purged HS2 assembly against every category of site a landing pad should avoid: all 9,388 AUGUSTUS-predicted genes (ab initio, Chlorella species model — a proper gene prediction, not the redundant Trinity-transcript mapping an earlier pass of this analysis mistakenly relied on), all 30 confirmed HS2/ALE candidate mutations, both confirmed structural variants, 3,014 repeat-artifact-flagged positions, 11,928 self-synteny duplicated blocks, all 76 positions unstable across the 2015-vs-2026 decade comparison, and the first/last 5kb of every contig — each with a buffer zone. Only 14 windows ≥3kb survive — the six largest, on contigs large enough to trust:

Revised after a proper gene prediction — and that revision is the finding

An earlier pass of this analysis, screening only against the 1,495 universal BUSCO essential genes, found windows up to 95 kb and flagged that it couldn't rule out overlap with non-essential genes. Running AUGUSTUS ab initio prediction (9,388 genes genome-wide, a realistic count for this genome size) to close that gap changed the answer substantially: the largest genuinely clear window anywhere in the HS2 genome is 12.7 kb, and among candidates on well-assembled, non-fragmentary contigs, none exceed 7.3 kb. That's not a failure of the search — it's a real property of this genome. A ~51 Mb chlorophyte assembly with 9,388 predicted genes is gene-dense enough that long gene-free intergenic stretches are simply scarce. Practically: a landing pad here should plan around a construct that fits in single-digit kilobases, not tens of kilobases, and ptg000046l:190,455–197,760 (7.3 kb, mid-contig on a 595 kb contig) is the best-supported starting candidate.

12 — Why it matters

From lab bench to bioprocess: what the advantage is worth

Genotype explains the mechanism; phenotype proves it works. The last question is whether it's worth anything outside a flask. A screening-level techno-economic and carbon-footprint analysis, built from the same measured biomass yields above and literature-anchored unit costs, says yes — concentrated exactly where it should be.

Scope, stated plainly

Both models cover cultivation only — feedstock and illumination, no harvesting, downstream processing, or capital cost. They are screening-level estimates built to show where the strain-productivity advantage does and doesn't translate into economic or environmental value, not a bankable process design.
