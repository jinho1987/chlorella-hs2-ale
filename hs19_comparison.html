HS19 vs. HS2: variant calling, corrected and doubly audited

HS19 is a separate Chlorella sp. HS2-derived mutant line, selected under sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl/bleach) exposure — a different stressor from ALE's hydrolysate adaptation, sharing only the HS2 reference and pipeline infrastructure. This page documents the full, current-reference reanalysis: a funnel bug that let 20 of 95 candidates through as false positives, an independent-assembly cross-check of the reference itself, and what survives of the coding mutations once both are applied — including protein and RNA structural analysis of every candidate that changes a protein sequence.

01

Selection pressure & why HS19 is analyzed separately

HS19's defining selective pressure was NaOCl treatment — an oxidative, chlorinating stress that damages proteins, membranes, and DNA through reactive chlorine/oxygen species. This is mechanistically distinct from ALE's hydrolysate-derived stress (furanic compounds, organic acids, osmotic/salinity) and from HMF's single-compound stress, both discussed on the main project page. All three mutant lines share the HS2 reference genome and pipeline infrastructure but are analyzed independently throughout — shared infrastructure does not imply shared biology.

Candidate functional categories considered for NaOCl adaptation

Oxidative/chlorine-radical detoxification (catalase, SOD, glutathione system, peroxiredoxin, thioredoxin) · DNA repair (glycosylases, mismatch repair, recombinases) · Chaperones/heat-shock proteins · Membrane and cell-wall remodeling · Efflux/ABC transporters · Photosystem II D1 repair machinery.

03

The funnel's second bug: a read-level allele-frequency audit

The "clear of standing HS2 heterozygosity" step above checks whether a variant is called in each baseline's VCF. That is not the same as checking whether the alt allele is actually present in the baseline's raw reads — a real allele sitting below the genotyper's calling threshold (typically needing roughly 20%+ support to trigger a confident heterozygous call) is invisible to a VCF-presence check even though it shows up plainly in a pileup.

This was checked directly: exact alt-allele read counts were pulled with samtools mpileup at all 95 candidate positions, in HS19 and all three HS2 baselines independently, parsing SNV, insertion, and deletion signals separately.

This audit has been built into the funnel itself rather than left as a one-off script: the raw-pileup check above now runs automatically, and the old homopolymer-only indel filter (single-base runs ≥6bp) has been replaced with a general short-tandem-repeat filter (mono–hexanucleotide repeat units, ≥3 repetitions) — STR contexts broadly, not just homopolymers, were the dominant false-positive source. Re-run from scratch: 71 final candidates, superseding both the original 95 and the 75 from the manual pass above (the improved STR filter alone catches several of the manually-flagged candidates, including the g5858 frameshift below, automatically). The missense set relevant to folding is unaffected: g3355 and g8574 remain confirmed, g1079 remains downgraded, g5894 and g5858 are now excluded by the pipeline itself.

What the flagged 20 look like

Most are indels in di-/tri-nucleotide short-tandem-repeat contexts (GGT>G, GCA>G, AAC>A-style calls) — a repeat-slippage signature affecting every sample, including HS2 itself, that the existing homopolymer filter didn't catch because it only screens single-base runs ≥6bp, not short tandem repeats generally. A few are SNPs (g5894, g4674) with graded background across the three baselines rather than a clean on/off signal — more consistent with standing variation shifting gradually than a discrete new mutation.

This is a targeted correctness check at candidates already identified, not a from-scratch re-calling, and it has not yet been extended to ALE's or HMF's candidate funnels — both use structurally similar baseline-subtraction logic and could plausibly show the same failure mode. Worth checking before treating those lists as immune to it.

04

NICEM independent-assembly reference cross-check

HS19 has no independent long-read assembly of its own, so it cannot be cross-validated the way ALE's candidates were against NICEM's independent ALE assembly. What can be checked is whether our HS2 reference itself is trustworthy at each candidate position, by comparing it to NICEM's separately-assembled HS2 genome. If our reference had an error at a given position, an "HS19 mutation" called there could be an artifact of the reference, not a real HS19-specific change.

A first attempt, using simple linear interpolation across whole-genome alignment blocks to translate coordinates, produced an implausible 38% mismatch rate — traced to the interpolation's own imprecision inside blocks with internal indels, not real disagreement. The check was redone with an indel-tolerant anchor: a 25bp window of our sequence is located as an exact substring inside a wider window of NICEM's assembly before comparing bases.

Including all confirmed missense positions and the retracted frameshift site. Our HS2 reference is not the source of any signal, real or spurious, discussed on this page. The naive first-pass result is reported here rather than discarded — a useful lesson that PAF-block linear interpolation isn't precise enough for single-base comparisons without an anchor-based correction step.

g3355 and g8574 show 40–54% allele fraction in HS19 (consistent with a real heterozygous call) and a clean 0% in every baseline. g1079's two adjacent substitutions (3bp apart in the genome, 1 codon apart in the protein, same haplotype) show only ~10% allele fraction in HS19 itself — too weak for a confident heterozygous call. g5894 shows a graded background rising smoothly across all three baselines into HS19, inconsistent with a discrete event. g5858 sits in a GT-repeat microsatellite tract at very low depth (7–18× vs. ~108× genome-wide) with both insertion and deletion signals scattered across every sample — including one baseline showing the identical insertion HS19 has — a repeat-instability signature, not a confident novel frameshift.

Both original ABC-transporter "hits" are also flagged

A keyword search against six NaOCl-relevant functional categories found only two non-generic hits — g3459 and g4674, both ABC-transporter family homologs. Both are also among the 20 candidates flagged by the allele-frequency audit (g3459: 14.3% in HS19 vs. 5–7% in baselines; g4674: 41% vs. 8–9%/0%). Neither is a clean, discrete HS19-specific call. After both rounds of correction, the NaOCl functional-category search's honest bottom line is a clean negative across the core oxidative-detox, DNA-repair, chaperone, and PSII-repair categories.

What actually survives

Of six sites folded, only g3355 and g8574 are confirmed real HS19-specific missense mutations — and neither shows a notable structural or RNA signal beyond being real. The site with the most striking computational signal (g1079) has the weakest genetic support; the two sites with a plausible functional story (g5894's cell-wall link, g5858's novel truncation) are the two that don't survive read-level scrutiny at all.

The 4 large, multi-gene calls fail a basic depth sanity check

A real homozygous deletion should approach 0× depth across its span; a real duplication should show roughly 2× the flanking depth. None of the four large (27.5–50kb) calls show either signature — not treated as confirmed. Most likely Delly's paired-end/coverage evidence responding to genuinely patchy short-read coverage (baseline depth itself varies 85–154× across these same regions) rather than four real events removing or doubling 4–11 complete genes. The 6 small events (4 short deletions, 2 breakends) are more plausible single-caller candidates.

Confirms the earlier depth-based finding: HS19 does not carry either ALE-specific SV

Zero HS19-specific calls of any type fall near ALE's 16kb duplication or its ~1.5kb insertion. An actual independent SV caller now agrees with the earlier raw-depth check, reinforcing the zero-convergence result at the SNV/indel level too.

08

Convergence with ALE and HMF

Using the final, hardened 71-candidate set: 0 shared positions, 0 shared genes with ALE's 24 confirmed candidates (21 genes), and 0 shared genes with HMF's own hardened set (which dropped from 7 to just 1 confirmed candidate under the same fix — see the main convergence section). Three independent stress-adaptation experiments on the same HS2 background, three non-overlapping mutation sets — see the main convergence section for the full three-way comparison including HMF vs. HS19 directly.

A mutation-spectrum signature that still matches the stressor's chemistry

HS19's spectrum remains C>A-dominant (a classic 8-oxoguanine oxidative-damage signature, mechanistically coherent with NaOCl exposure) after the audit correction — distinct from ALE's T>G/T>C-dominant spectrum. This finding is unaffected by the missense retractions above, since it's computed from the full corrected SNV set, not just the coding subset.

09

Comparison to the prior (BioTo) analysis

BioTo's 2025-09-11 report analyzed the same comparison using a ~52Mb/52-scaffold filtered subset of the seeders.co.kr assembly and SAMtools v0.1.16 (a 2011-era caller). Two points of agreement and one caution, unaffected by everything above:

  • Agreement — SV calling. BioTo's three-caller analysis (BreakDancer, Pindel, Delly) found nothing confirmed by all three, matching this project's own finding that raw short-read SV signal is dominated by a known-unreliable inversion-calling artifact.
  • Caution — SNP calling. BioTo's 99% heterozygous SNP call rate is very likely a caller artifact given this project's own finding that HS2's true heterozygosity is ~0.0036%.
  • Mapping quality. HS19 reads map at 97.89% to this project's complete reference vs. BioTo's 93.18% against their smaller reference.
10

Summary verdict

What holds up
  • The HS2 reference itself is independently validated by NICEM's separately-assembled genome — zero mismatches at every resolvable candidate position.
  • 71 final HS19-specific SNV/indel candidates (hardened v3 pipeline), absent from all 3 HS2 baselines both by formal genotype call and by raw read support, with a general short-tandem-repeat filter replacing the original homopolymer-only check.
  • Two confirmed missense mutations (g3355 P1044L, g8574 R562Q), both structurally and computationally unremarkable but genuinely real.
  • Zero convergence with ALE or HMF at position or gene level, holding across two independent correction passes now — three separately-evolved lines on the same background, three genuinely distinct routes.
  • A mutation-spectrum signature (C>A-dominant) mechanistically consistent with NaOCl's oxidative chemistry.
Honestly still open
  • Two of six originally-reported protein-altering mutations are retracted (g5894, g5858) and one is downgraded (g1079) — this page exists specifically to keep that correction visible rather than silently fixed.
  • HS19 has short-read data only; no independent long-read assembly exists to cross-validate its calls the way ALE's were against NICEM's ALE assembly. The NICEM cross-check here validates the reference, not HS19's specific calls.
  • The same VCF-presence-vs-raw-read-support gap that produced this correction has not yet been checked in ALE's or HMF's candidate funnels.
  • With only 2 confirmed missense mutations, the NaOCl functional-category search is operating on a genuinely thin base — underpowered by construction, independent of whether the search itself is sound.
